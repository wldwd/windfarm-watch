The process

Approval tracks and their windows

The tracks run in parallel, not one after another. Planning follows one of two routes depending on when the DA was lodged. Times are statutory or typical periods.

WAPC significant development

Required for wind farms of $20m+ lodged from 5 Oct 2026

  1. Pre-lodgement engagementCompulsory. The Shire is involved.

  3. at least 28 daysAdvertised for public commentPlans online, local newspaper notice, sign on site and letters to nearby owners. The Shire separately gets 60 days to comment.

Ground rules

Worth knowing

No neighbour appeals on planning

In WA only the developer can take a planning decision to the State Administrative Tribunal. The public's say is the comment window and the meeting.

The 7-day EPA window is final

If the EPA decides not to assess a proposal, nobody can appeal. The referral comment is the only say on whether a full assessment happens.

1.5 km from homes

The Shire's wind farm policy sets 1.5 km from the nearest existing dwelling or approved building envelope, and turbines closer than that are referred to the EPA. The draft state code also expects about 1,500 m, set by noise modelling.

Night noise limit

35 dB at a rural home at night, under the Environmental Protection (Noise) Regulations 1997, whatever the planning approval says.

Federal rules change on 1 Dec 2026

Referrals from that date use new pathways, and a WA–Commonwealth assessment agreement could merge some steps.
